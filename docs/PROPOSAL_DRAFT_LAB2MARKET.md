## 1. Aim & Objectives

### Aim
To strengthen the role of Technology Transfer Offices (TTOs) in bringing research and innovation to the market by providing AI-powered decision-support tools, training and methodological guidelines that enable data-driven commercialisation workflows.

### Alignment with Expected Outcomes (Call)
- Enhanced TTO capacity for commercialisation and market-readiness assessment
- Increased uptake of digital/AI tools in technology transfer
- Cross-border sharing of best practices and methodologies

### Objectives (by end of project)
| # | Objective | Aligned to |
|---|-----------|------------|
| O1 | Deliver a Commercialisation Support Platform (prototype) used by ≥2 TTOs | Expected outcome: strengthened TTO capabilities |
| O2 | Produce training modules and deliver workshops to TTO staff on AI-assisted commercialisation | Expected outcome: skills development |
| O3 | Document pilot results and create guidelines for AI-driven commercialisation workflows | Expected outcome: dissemination, replication |

## 4. Methodology

### Overarching Approach
We use an **iterative, user-centred methodology** combining (1) co-design with TTOs, (2) adaptation of proven commercial intelligence methods (Performance+), and (3) agile delivery with validation loops.

### Scientific and Technical Rationale
- **Co-design:** TTOs define real priorities and workflows. We avoid building generic tools; instead we adapt to their context (portfolio types, sector, maturity). This increases uptake and relevance.
- **Adaptation over greenfield:** The Performance+ methodology (strategy weights, portfolio scoring, AI recommendations) has been validated in B2B commercial contexts. Adapting it for innovation portfolios reduces risk and accelerates delivery. The core logic — prioritise by multiple criteria, propose actions, align stakeholders — translates to TTO use cases (prioritise innovations, recommend commercial pathways, align researchers and industry).
- **Agile delivery:** Short cycles (design → build → pilot → feedback) allow adjustment. We do not lock requirements at day one; we validate with real TTO data and iterate.

### Concepts and Assumptions
- **Assumption 1:** TTOs manage heterogeneous portfolios (patents, spin-offs, licences) that can be scored and prioritised using configurable criteria (market potential, TRL, resource needs).
- **Assumption 2:** AI (generative models) can produce useful, contextual recommendations when given structured input (portfolio data, strategy parameters).
- **Assumption 3:** Training increases adoption; tools alone are insufficient without capacity building.

### Challenges & Mitigations
| Challenge | Mitigation |
|-----------|------------|
| TTOs have limited time for new tools | Co-design keeps scope lean; training is concise; focus on high-impact workflows |
| Data sensitivity (portfolio info) | On-premise or controlled cloud; anonymised aggregates for guidelines |
| AI outputs may be generic | Use domain-specific prompts; feed structured TTO data; human-in-the-loop validation |
| Geographic/cultural differences | Pilots in 2–3 countries; guidelines capture variance; flexible configuration |

### Why This Approach Over Alternatives
- **Alternative A (generic TTO software):** Off-the-shelf tools often lack AI and customisation. We deliver tailored, AI-enhanced support.
- **Alternative B (pure research):** The call is Coordination and Support — we focus on deployment and uptake, not novel algorithms.
- **Alternative C (training only):** Tools without training underutilise; training without tools has limited lasting effect. We combine both.
